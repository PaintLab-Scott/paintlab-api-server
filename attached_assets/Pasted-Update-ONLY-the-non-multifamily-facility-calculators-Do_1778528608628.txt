Primary goal:
Fix non-multifamily calculator pricing so touch-up scopes are not overpriced, while also applying transparent monthly minimum managed-service pricing.

Important business logic:
For non-multifamily calculators, the selected service type should calculate the actual customer-facing repaint cost for that service cycle.

Definitions:
- Full Repaint = 100% of the selected calculated surface coverage
- Touch-Up = targeted repaint maintenance of the selected calculated touch-up surface coverage
- Touch-up cost factor = customer-facing cost percentage of a full repaint for the selected touch-up surface
- Service frequency = number of times per year that selected scope is serviced
- Monthly minimum = managed-service pricing floor used when the calculated monthly price is below the operational minimum

Do NOT apply extra tier multipliers.
Do NOT apply extra operational multipliers.
Do NOT multiply by touch-up coverage percentage twice.
Do NOT replace the calculation logic with arbitrary plan pricing.

Core pricing logic:

For each selected zone/scope:

If service type = Full Repaint:
zoneServiceCycleCost = zoneSurfaceCoverageSqft * facilityFullRepaintPssfRate

If service type = Touch-Up:
zoneServiceCycleCost = zoneSurfaceCoverageSqft * facilityFullRepaintPssfRate * facilityTouchUpCostFactor

Important:
If the calculator already calculates and displays reduced touch-up surface coverage, do NOT multiply by touchUpCoveragePercent again.

Example:
Retail assumptions:
- Full repaint PSSF = $1.35
- Touch-up coverage = 18%
- Touch-up cost factor = 45%
- Displayed touch-up surface coverage = 351 sqft

Correct touch-up service cycle cost:
351 * 1.35 * 0.45 = $213.23

Incorrect:
351 * 1.35 * 0.18 * 0.45

The 351 sqft already reflects the 18% touch-up coverage.

Total service cycle cost:
totalServiceCycleCost = sum(zoneServiceCycleCost for all selected zones/scopes)

Tier frequency logic:

Annual Shield:
serviceVisitsPerYear = 1
calculatedAnnualCost = totalServiceCycleCost * 1
calculatedMonthlyPrice = calculatedAnnualCost / 12

Bi-Annual Shield:
serviceVisitsPerYear = 2
calculatedAnnualCost = totalServiceCycleCost * 2
calculatedMonthlyPrice = calculatedAnnualCost / 12

Quarterly Guard:
serviceVisitsPerYear = 4
calculatedAnnualCost = totalServiceCycleCost * 4
calculatedMonthlyPrice = calculatedAnnualCost / 12

Monthly minimums for non-multifamily calculators:

Annual Shield minimum = $99/mo
Bi-Annual Shield minimum = $199/mo
Quarterly Guard minimum = $399/mo

Minimum pricing logic:

For each tier:
if calculatedMonthlyPrice < tierMonthlyMinimum:
  minimumApplied = true
  displayMonthlyPrice = tierMonthlyMinimum
else:
  minimumApplied = false
  displayMonthlyPrice = calculatedMonthlyPrice

Do NOT hide pricing when the minimum is applied.
Always show the final displayed monthly price.

Pricing card display rules:

Each pricing card should show:
1. Final displayed monthly price prominently
2. Whether the price is based on selected scope or minimum managed-service pricing

If minimumApplied = true, show directly under the monthly price:
“Minimum managed-service price”

Also show:
“Calculated scope price: $X/mo”

Example:
Annual Shield
$99/mo
Minimum managed-service price
Calculated scope price: $18/mo

If minimumApplied = false, show directly under the monthly price:
“Based on selected scope”

User notification logic:

If one or more tier minimums are applied, show a subtle notice directly above the pricing cards.

Notice headline:
“Minimum service pricing applied”

Notice body:
“Your selected scope is below the managed-service minimum for one or more plans. You can continue with the minimum plan price or add more repaint scope to see pricing based fully on your selected work.”

Optional supporting sentence:
“Minimums help cover scheduling, coordination, recurring availability, and professionally managed execution.”

This notice should appear only when at least one tier has minimumApplied = true.

If all tiers exceed their minimums, hide this notice.

Important:
This notice is not an error state.
Do not make it red.
Do not make it feel punitive.
Style it as a helpful, transparent, professional explanation.
